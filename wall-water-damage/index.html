Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Charleston

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. When the wall is soft, crumbling, moldy or was soaked by dirty water, we make a flood cut, a straight line above the damage, and remove everything below it.

Signs to Look For

  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Soft or crumbly spots you notice when pushing lightly on the wall
  • New gaps along casings and baseboard tops as swollen material shifts
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • A musty smell that is stronger near one wall or outlet
What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Walls You Cannot See

Drywall hidden in back of cabinetry and built-in units tends to be missed and dries very slowly. Checking under the cabinet base and on the reverse face tells us if it is wet.

Mold Inside Cavities

Mold thrives on the rear face of drywall because it is paper covered, dark and has no moving air. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

Water in a perimeter wall may reach the sheathing on the far side of the cavity. It gets a moisture reading prior to closing, since it holds dampness a long time.

Save or Replace

What Stays and What Goes

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Moisture enters where the panel meets the floor and wicks upward from there. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

The wet line rises higher, baseboards swell and paint begins to blister. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Days 3 through 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. A musty odor grows stronger while the batts settle and mold covers more of the unseen face.

After a week

Damage Reaches the Studs

By this point the studs and plates have been damp long enough for mold and early rot. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.

What We Do

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Using meter readings and thermal images, our team outlines where the water went, upward and sideways, on both faces of the wall and in neighboring rooms.

2

Decide What Stays

You get a straight answer on what can be dried where it is and what must be removed, judged by the category of water, the time it sat and the firmness of the drywall.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Where the drywall stays, the trim is pulled and airflow is sent inside the wall. On sections coming out, a straight flood cut is made and soaked insulation is removed.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
